# Drex in the Stalk Market workflow (research, 1 Nov 2026)

## What Drex is, and what we've seen so far

Drex (`DREX_API_KEY`, `https://drex.nace.ai/v1/systemone`, model `drex-latest`) is a decision API with the TypeSafe System One schema.
We send a **state**: a description of the situation, without the ticker or date, so it can't recall what happened next.
We also send **typed questions**: `noul` asks for the probability a statement is true, `score` asks for a rating on a scale we define.
It answers in about 1.7 s. It does no browsing and holds no memory: it judges only what we give it.

**First test (31 Oct, `scripts/drex_score.py`):** we asked "will this trade hit its target before its stop?" on 77 backtest trades.
Its Brier score was 0.091, against 0.083 for always guessing the average hit rate (lower is better), so it did no better than guessing.
It rated every trade below 50%.

**Why that test was weak (found today):** the state we sent (`stalk/llm/drex.py::setup_state`) held only the side, the strategy name,
the time and our own uncalibrated Time Machine odds. The stop description is also stale ("1.5 ATR, 5-minute bars").
Drex had almost nothing to judge. That's our mistake, not proof that Drex can't help.

## How Drex can help place trades: a factor checklist, with veto power only

Drex should not decide trades. It should check each queued setup against named factors, using a state rich enough to judge,
and be allowed to **block** a trade when the factors are against it. The rules and the risk gate stay in charge of placing, sizing and exits.

**1. A richer state for each setup** (all from data we already have, or can fetch for free):
- the trade: side, entry, stop and target in % from entry, risk ₹, the strategy's thesis in one line;
- the stock right now: RSI(14), distance from VWAP, today's move vs its average daily range, relative volume, gap at the open,
  where price sits in today's range, distance from the previous day's high and low, 52-week high distance;
- the market: NIFTY's and BANKNIFTY's move today, whether they agree, India VIX level (Yahoo `^INDIAVIX`);
- the sector: the sector name and how the sector's stocks are moving today;
- valuation (as context, not a trigger): PE vs its own history and the sector (Yahoo fundamentals);
- news: today's headlines for the stock and whether results or a board meeting fall within 2 days (NSE corporate announcements).
  Headlines are passed as text, and Drex judges whether they support or contradict the trade.

**2. A fixed checklist of questions** (each `noul`, so every answer is a probability we can score):
- "The trade is in the direction of the broader market today."
- "No scheduled event (results, board meeting) in the next 2 sessions."
- "Today's headlines for this stock don't contradict the direction of the trade."
- "The move so far today leaves room to reach the target within the session."
- "The setup matches the strategy's thesis" (the thesis text is in the state).
And one `score`: overall setup quality, from 1 (clearly against) to 5 (strong and aligned).

**3. What the engine does with the answers:**
- every answer is stored on the setup and the trade (extending today's `drex_p`);
- **veto rule:** block the trade if any checklist item is under 0.3, or the score is ≤ 2. The block reason shows on the Desk ("Drex: results tomorrow");
- vetoed setups are still tracked as "would have" trades, so their outcomes can be measured;
- Drex never places, sizes or changes a trade, and live settings never change from its answers.

**4. "Modifying the conditions"** happens through the experiment process, not automatically:
- if, over 20+ setups, one checklist item clearly separates winners from losers (e.g. "headlines contradict the trade" loses 70% of the time),
  that item becomes a fixed rule in RULES.md after one pre-registered day-by-day test;
- items that don't separate anything get dropped. Every change is logged in RUNBOOK with the trials count.

## How we'd test it (no real money involved)

1. Rebuild `setup_state` with the factors above and the checklist questions (one change: the state and questions).
2. Replay a month day by day (`scripts/day_by_day.py`) with Drex answering each queued setup. Drex doesn't see the ticker or the date, so it can't look up what happened.
3. Compare three things over the month:
   - Brier per checklist item vs the base rate;
   - P&L of trades it allowed vs trades it vetoed;
   - the account with the veto on vs off.
4. Keep the veto only if it removes more losers than winners on unseen days. Otherwise it stays a logged opinion.

Cost: about 5–10 calls a day at one trade a day plus shortlisted setups, each about 1.7 s. The key is already in `.env`.

## Not Drex: Dexter (noted only)

[virattt/dexter](https://github.com/virattt/dexter) is a different tool: an open-source financial-research agent (MIT, TypeScript)
that browses, reads filings and checks its own answers. It can't trade, needs an OpenAI key plus a paid Financial Datasets key,
and its data looks US-centred. It could later fetch the news and fundamentals that feed Drex's state. Not planned now.
