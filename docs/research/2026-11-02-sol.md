1. How would you define "market mood" for an intraday desk on NSE, using only price, breadth, India VIX and index data?

**Sol:**
My honest read: you’ve improved trade selection, but you haven’t yet demonstrated an edge.

1. **“Mood” should mean a regime, not confidence.** I’d use three axes:
   - **Direction:** index versus VWAP, VWAP slope, and whether breadth is expanding with the move.
   - **Structure:** persistent movement with shallow retracements versus repeated VWAP crossings and failed breaks.
   - **Volatility:** today’s opening range against its historical distribution, plus India VIX level and change.

   That gives you rising trend, falling trend, rotation, or disorder. Label it using information available at the decision time, then update it. VIX describes the volatility backdrop; it doesn’t supply direction. Keep the definitions few and fixed.

2. **First, reconcile capital, risk and charges.** At a 0.55% stop, ₹10,000 deployed without leverage risks roughly ₹55 before costs—not ₹100. Your ₹10 charge is therefore about 18% of actual price risk. That’s a serious hurdle.

   I’d examine net expectancy by regime and setup before changing frequency. Include realistic fills and slippage. Keep only opportunities whose measured advantage clears that hurdle. “Two or three we’re sure of” is not a selection rule, and increasing turnover could simply multiply the drag.

3. **The mistake is treating better explanations as stronger evidence.** Index, breadth and sector agreement may largely measure the same thing. Stop robustness is useful, but it doesn’t establish independence from your tuning.

   ₹226 over three months is encouraging, not convincing. Freeze the rules and test forward. Your rising-month weakness deserves investigation, but don’t automatically patch it: the patch becomes another fitted strategy.

The desk’s job is to identify conditions with positive net expectancy. Feeling sure is optional.

**Sol:**
I’d pick **(c), specifically a larger paper account trading the same cash equities**. That preserves the instrument and lets you size around a defensible stop.

But distinguish fixing the economics from proving the strategy. More capital only helps if position size increases and costs grow slower than risk. Recalculate brokerage, taxes and slippage at that size; ₹10 won’t necessarily stay ₹10.

I wouldn’t widen the stop just to spend ₹100 of risk. The stop should reflect where the trade thesis fails. And 2× leverage fixes the sizing mismatch, but it cannot rescue zero gross expectancy. Changing instruments also means testing a new strategy: liquidity, costs and price behaviour change.

For the second signal, my candidate is **unusual stock-specific participation**: cumulative volume relative to that stock’s usual volume *at the same time of day*. Pair the market regime with evidence that this particular stock is attracting exceptional activity.

Volume isn’t automatically independent, and heavy trading doesn’t tell you direction. Let price define the entry; use participation to test whether otherwise identical trend entries have better **net** outcomes.

The crucial experiment is conditional: among sessions already classified as trending, does unusual participation improve results on untouched data? Compare it with ordinary participation, controlling for time and volatility.

That’s the standard I’d apply to every additional filter: incremental predictive value after the first signal, rather than a different-looking indicator.
